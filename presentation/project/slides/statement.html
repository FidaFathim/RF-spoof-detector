Problem statement

A CNN that identifies radios from their hardware flaws can be fooled by small crafted perturbations. In our tests, PGD fooled it 93 to 100% of the time. Can an independent, physics-based second check reject forged signals without knowing the attack in advance?

QUESTION 1

Does a separate check catch attacks that already fooled the CNN?

QUESTION 2

Is it better than CNN confidence, or than a single feature?

QUESTION 3

What does it cost in rejected genuine signals?

RF spoof detection · 2

State the problem as a question. Radio fingerprinting authenticates a transmitter from its hardware flaws using a CNN, but a small crafted perturbation can make the CNN accept a forged signal; in our tests PGD fooled it 93 to 100 percent of the time. Our question is whether a second, independent check based on hardware physics can reject those forgeries without being told about the attack. We split it into three questions: does it catch attacks that already fooled the CNN, is it better than CNN confidence or a single feature, and what does it cost in wrongly rejected genuine signals. The rest of the talk answers these in order.
