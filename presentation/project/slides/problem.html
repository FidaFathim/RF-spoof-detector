INTRODUCTION

Radios can be recognised by their hardware flaws, and those flaws can be faked

1 IDENTIFY

Every transmitter has tiny, unique imperfections. A CNN learns to recognise them from raw I/Q samples.

2 AUTHENTICATE

The receiver trusts a signal only if the CNN says it came from the device it claims to be.

3 ATTACK

A tiny crafted perturbation makes the CNN confidently say "trusted device".

In our tests, gradient attacks fooled the CNN 93 to 100% of the time.

RF spoof detection · 3

Set up the threat. RF fingerprinting identifies a transmitter from imperfections in its hardware, not from a password. A CNN learns those imperfections. The weakness: the CNN can be attacked. The three attacks we used are FGSM, PGD and UAP; they add a tiny, carefully computed change to the signal. With a strong enough setting, PGD fooled the CNN 93 to 100 percent of the time in our experiments. That is the problem we respond to.
